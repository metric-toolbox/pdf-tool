How to Compress a PDF to Under 1MB for Free (2026)

By the FreePDFHub Team · Last updated: October 31, 2026

Job portals, government forms and email systems love one phrase: “Maximum file size: 1MB.” Meanwhile your scanned PDF is 8MB. Here's how to get any PDF under the limit, free, without installing anything.

The 30-Second Method

  1. Open the free PDF compressor — no sign-up needed.
  2. Drop in your PDF.
  3. Select Extreme (smallest size).
  4. Click Compress and download the result.

For most scanned documents this cuts 70–90% of the size immediately.

Understanding Compression Levels

  • Low compression: near-original quality. Use for portfolios, print, design proofs.
  • Recommended: the balance point — crisp text, big savings. Use for email and most uploads.
  • Extreme: smallest files. Text stays readable; photos get softer. Use when a hard 1MB limit applies.

Why Is My PDF So Big in the First Place?

Scans: a phone-scanned page is a full photo — often 1–3MB per page. Embedded photos: a single uncompressed image can dwarf the rest of the document. High-DPI exports: design tools export at print resolution by default. Compression re-encodes those images efficiently, which is where the huge savings come from.

If One Pass Isn't Enough

  • Remove unneeded pages first with the Split tool — fewer pages, smaller file.
  • Run Extreme twice. A second pass on an already-compressed file can squeeze out a bit more.
  • Rescan smarter: scan in grayscale at 150 DPI instead of color at 600 DPI.

Is It Safe for Sensitive Documents?

On FreePDFHub, yes: compression runs entirely in your browser, so bank statements and ID scans are never uploaded to any server — a key difference from most compressor sites.

FAQ

Will the receiver notice the compression?

At Recommended level, rarely. At Extreme, photos look slightly softer but text remains perfectly readable.

Can I compress without losing any quality at all?

True lossless savings on scans are tiny. Visually-lossless (Low level) is the realistic option.
